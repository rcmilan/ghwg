# Ghost Hotel — vertical slice

A hotel run in two Dimensions at once: human guests pay rent in the real one, ghost guests haunt the same Rooms in the ghost one, and the Concierge matches them, serves, cleans and grows the hotel.

Run from this directory:

```powershell
go run .
```

The executable's icon, a Shadow Person's black head, is `icon.ico`, linked in through `rsrc_windows_amd64.syso`; after changing the icon, rebuild that with `windres --preprocessor=cat -O coff -F pe-x86-64 icon.rc -o rsrc_windows_amd64.syso`.

## Controls

`WASD` moves the concierge; `Space` opens the reception terminal (from the computer's own column, behind or in front of the counter), a web store, and at the mirror on the lobby wall shifts the whole hotel between the real and the ghost dimension (through one of nine random 0.8 s transitions), on a room's doormat delivers the item it asked for in the real dimension (with nothing to deliver there it opens the hotel view; the mat cell west of the door is kept for the guest card), at the shelf of the item you hold puts it back; on the mat cell west of a room's door, under its plate (a brass plaque), it opens the guest's card (`TALK` shows on the right; see "Matching" below); inside a room it cleans the dirt of the dimension you are in; anywhere it has nothing else to do, `Space` opens the hotel view; whatever SPACE would act on glows while you stand in its range, and a `!` by the terminal's screen means something on sale and affordable that the terminal hasn't shown you yet this run (it goes once you see its tab); `Esc` closes whatever is open in one press (the hotel view steps back one level), and with nothing open pauses (Resume / Quit to title); on the title it quits. Hold `T` to run the whole world ×4, guests, staff, Shadows, animations and the clock together (dev aid). Dev flag: `-scale 0.25` runs the world at a quarter speed, smoothly (any factor; `T` still runs it ×4). A person's game is guarded: if one frame runs over 3 s or the game holds over 2 GB it quits and writes why to `ghosthotel-guard.log` in the temp folder; the world never takes more than 4 ms of a frame, it runs slower instead.

## How it plays

The hotel runs on a real-time clock, one in-game day in about 4 minutes, starting on Day 1 at 08:00. The hotel has two rooms side by side. Guests arrive already booked through an app, from the street during the day (none between 02:00 and 06:00, most from 14:00 to 22:00), and walk straight to their room; one comes only while a room it fits is empty. A party is one guest or a pair; a pair walks together, the second one a step behind, and needs a twin or double room. Every room opens as a Standard single ($125 a night); twin and double beds ($200) come by changing a room's type (see "Room types" below), and the Deluxe tier is a later unlock. The terminal (the store) pauses the world; a priest is called for a room from the hotel view. Each guest stays 1–3 nights and is a homebody, who stays in, or a tourist, who goes out each morning (08:00–10:00) from the day after it arrives and comes back at night (19:00–22:00); the room stays booked while they're out. A door opens to whoever is near: the concierge, the courier and a priest open any room's door, a guest only its own, and a boarded room's stays shut to everyone. The concierge may walk in on guests (the darkness lifts while he is in), at a Mood cost for each game minute he stays. Once the whole party is inside with the door shut, the room fades into darkness; it clears only while its door is open, and stays dark while a tourist is out. The light follows the clock: clear by day, warm at dusk, deep blue at night under the corridors' ceiling lights and the light falling from the windows, and pink at dawn; the kitchen keeps its own lights on all night, and a lantern beam goes ahead of the concierge and every staff member at night, only where it is dark: not in the lit lobby, the kitchen or a room the concierge lights up. Living characters cast a dim shadow at their feet; ghosts cast none and float a few pixels up, bobbing slowly, and so does the concierge while in the ghost dimension. Potted plants stand in the lobby's west corners and at each corridor's dead ends. The lobby stays lit except from 01:00 to 06:00, and an empty room lights up while the concierge is in it. Your money shows on the terminal. On the last morning, between 10:00 and 12:00, the guest checks out: the whole stay is paid as it steps out of its door (`+$X`, the tip, scaled by the room's Mood pay factor) and the room is free again. At 23:00 guests walk into their beds, where only their heads show above the blanket, and get up at 07:00; the concierge can lie in a bed too. A bed is entered and left by its side, never over its foot: a guest walks round from its spot at the foot to the free cell beside the bed; in a double, against the wall, the wall-side sleeper crosses the other half. Awake in their room, guests keep busy: they look out of the windows, watch TV, browse the bookshelf, open the wardrobe or drawers, check the mirror or sit on the cushion, rest on their bed, stand at its foot glancing around, and a pair now and then stops to chat face to face. Guests in their room ask for room service once or twice a day while awake (07:00–23:00): a white `!!` balloon appears by their head, always on screen, pinned to the nearest edge when they're out of view. Serve them in the real dimension (SPACE at their door): the room's Mood rises by 25 when you get there at once, by less the longer they wait; when the request expires unanswered the Mood drops by 10. Serving doesn't calm fear. In the ghost dimension the hotel turns arcade-colored, you see through the darkness into booked rooms. Fear (a red glow) comes only from a Shadow Person's sightings, and each point of it earns a point of Ectoplasm (shown in green and purple, and on the terminal); cleaning dirt in the ghost dimension earns it too. A guest frightened enough flees with the whole party, unpaid, and the room is free at once: they jump with an `AAAH!` and run out at triple speed. Every guest has a look of its own: hair, skin and clothes are recolored at random. Guests talk in speech balloons: `Hello!` on arrival, a greeting by the time of day when going out or coming back (then a pair chats between themselves), and now and then a line in their room: less cozy the lower the room's Mood. You hear guests only near the concierge, and in the real dimension not through walls; a guest asking for service says nothing else. Each room also has a Mood, from -100 (dread) to 100 (cozy): guests leave mess that lowers it. In the ghost dimension the whole room shifts toward green and purple as it falls (in the real one it only tints faintly green), and at -100 it pulses: the room is closed and no new guest comes to it. A priest called from the hotel view walks in and does a ritual: an opening line, a chant with a hop and rising sparkles, a burst of light, a closing line (see "Priests" below). Each Father is one person: while he is in the hotel, walking in, working or leaving, his call is not offered. A request waits 45 s. `TALK` is not offered while the party sleeps. The terminal no longer sells a Shadow Person (the Hex is gone too); a Shadow (dev: `-shadow`) is a black figure that dashes along a room's back wall while guests are in, more often the lower the room's Mood. Each run frightens a guest who sees it (it hops and cries out) more than one who doesn't, and the shadow keeps the room uneasy, but it never scares anyone into fleeing. Father M banishes it.

## Room Mood

It is the one number that counts. It moves only on events and never drifts: serving a request raises it (+25, less the longer they waited), an expired request lowers it (-10), guests' mess, a frightened guest and the concierge lingering inside lower it, and cleaning raises it to +50 (a clean never lowers it). Dirt spots are Mood made visible: none from +50 up, then one for about every 3.75 points down to -100. Cleaning a room (SPACE on a stain, its guests in or not) wipes all its spots and, in the ghost dimension, banks half the dirt as Ectoplasm. Pay follows Mood through the Mood pay factor, 0 at -100, 1 at 0 and 2 at +100 (linear): it scales the rent and the checkout tip. Rent is paid up front, on each guest's own clock: the moment a guest steps into its room it pays its share of that hour (the Rate per 24 game hours, split between the party) as a `+$N` at the door, then again every game hour from that entry, in the room or out; a new run's rooms start at Mood +50; the tip, one night's Rate whatever the stay's length, is paid at checkout, so a dirty room pays little and a cozy one pays double. There is no per-guest comfort any more. Ghosts will also lower it by haunting (dev code: `g.haunts(room, damage)`; a ghost's Haunt stat x is the gap between haunts, its Phantasm sets the damage). Dev flag: `-mood N` starts a run with every room at Mood N, standing in room 101 (use with `-shot`).

## Ghost guests

A run starts with the Mirror locked, humans only; the Mirror on the lobby wall is drawn covered and SPACE at it does nothing; buy it at the terminal ($15, hotel tab: about half a minute of the first room's rent), and once the courier carries it to the wall and installs it each room also books a ghost guest, next to its human one and independent of it: it walks in from the street on the ghost layer, one of the four characters redrawn as a flat arcade-ghost color by its kind (Wisp cyan, Specter pink, Wraith orange, Poltergeist red; which kind gets which is a placeholder), stays, sleeps and checks out like a guest. A ghost never bumps, yields or queues for a human, nor a human for a ghost. It pays no money, ignores dirt and asks for nothing; it only haunts: every few game hours in its room it lowers the room's Mood, by more the stronger it is (this is the first pressure on Mood; a closed room takes no ghost either). In the real dimension ghosts are not drawn. A ghost switches on the Shadow People of its room the moment it comes in, and they dash at once (2026-11-14: a ghost earns from the door, as a human pays from the door); a strong one (every kind but the Wisp) also switches on those of a corridor or the entrance hall it stands in: the same Shadow as ever: a black figure that fades in on that area's upper wall: in a room it dashes along the back wall while humans are in, as often as its ghost haunts, and shows only while it dashes (only a guest who sees the dash is frightened; in a corridor or the hall now and then), frightening the humans who see it as before (they hop and cry out, Fear rises and earns Ectoplasm, never into fleeing by itself), under the room's fog like the props; Father M banishes it until that ghost leaves; it fades when the ghost leaves. The mirror prop inside a room is a separate upgrade (K1) and has nothing to do with the lobby Mirror: only the lobby one is drawn covered. In the ghost dimension ghosts draw like guests (one flat color each, almost opaque) and humans as flat white silhouettes with a small white glow. The haunt values are deliberately harsh placeholders (rooms close fast and money falls) until X2 lets you move parties between rooms. In the sim table, the new `ghosts` column counts ghost bookings per run; only the `opener` Strategy buys the Mirror, so ghosts show for the others only with `-mirror`, which starts every run with it unlocked. Dev flag: `-ghosts N` starts a run with a human and a ghost of Phantasm N (1..12; 4 and up is strong) settled in room 101, you inside it (use with `-shot out.png -at 9300` for them asleep, and `-ghost` for the ghost dimension).

## The opening

Both rooms are closed and unowned: their doors are drawn boarded up, they book no guest of either dimension and the concierge cannot enter them, and the run starts with exactly $100, one Room key's worth, and two of each supply in the kitchen, enough for the first guest's requests; until a room is open the key is the only thing on sale, so no first buy can strand the run. The Mirror ($15) is bought from the first room's rent, in about the first minute. Room keys are the `Room key` action of a room in the hotel view (`Space` away from everything, key icon), paid at once, and the courier walks the key to that room's door; when he drops it the room is owned, its door unboards and its first guest books about half an hour later. Several keys can be bought at once, one a room, and the price rises with each room owned or already paid for: $100 for the first, $115 for the second, $130 for the third: 15% of the first key more for each room owned (`keyBase` and `priceStep`), so the first choice is which room to open and the second room is earned. The Mirror stays covered until bought ($15), so a run goes: key, first guests, Mirror, then save up for the next key. The pacing goal is to reach the Mirror (the SHIFT feature) early (see "Pacing" below). The opening: the title screen is the whole hotel in the ghost dimension, both rooms boarded, the concierge not there and ghosts strolling the street. `Space` brings three brief lines over it, one after the other on a timer (`Space` shows them all at once); `Space` again and the concierge walks in from the street like a guest while the view closes in to the game's own, and the world shifts to real as he steps into the entrance; the clock and the world wait for all of it. Dev flags: `-skipintro` skips the intro when playing; the sim and every dev start (`-shot`, `-shop`, ...) never play it unless asked: `-intro N` starts at the opening (`0` the title, `1` the three lines, `2` the walk in; use with `-shot out.png -at F`), and `-key N` starts a run that has already ordered the key of room N (`0` is the first), you in the corridor by its door (use with `-shot -at 285` for the courier at the door, `-at 950` for the first guest). The sim starts every run closed too; `-open` starts it with every room owned, as before.

## Room types

A room's type is something you change, one room at a time. Twin beds, then the Double bed, are unlocks in the terminal's HOTEL tab ($350 and $700, each on sale once the one before is in and a little more money is earned: Twin after the Mirror). `Change type` is an action of a room in the hotel view: it lists the unlocked beds, each with its fee ($50 Single, $75 Twin, $125 Double), and SPACE on one pays it at once. An empty room changes as soon as you close the terminal: it rolls like a slot machine's reel, the old room sliding out the bottom as the new one comes in from the top, in either dimension. A room in use is scheduled instead: it takes no new booking, human or ghost, until everyone has left (and the concierge and any priest are out of it), then rolls on its own, seen or not. A scheduled change shows on the room's card (`> TWIN`), and the room's `Change type` action becomes `CANCEL > TWIN`: SPACE cancels it for a full refund. The room keeps its Mood through a change; its dirt is spotted again on the new floor. Capacity and Rate follow the beds: a Twin or Double takes pairs. The in-Room Mirror (`Room mirror`, a room's action in the hotel view, 120 Ectoplasm, bought once) is paid at once and carried by the courier to that room's door, like a key; when he gets there it is hung on the room's back wall, guests in or not, and a change the room waits for keeps waiting; from then on every room that changes type, and every room a key opens, gets one too, and every change also costs 5 Ectoplasm. SPACE under a room's Mirror shifts the dimension like the lobby's. A boarded room can't be changed. All amounts are placeholders. The supplies live in the kitchen, the lobby's part east of the archway, walled off with an open doorway in its west wall: Water in the fridge, Food in the oven, Towels on the shelf, all against its back wall and solid. You fetch from the cell just below each, and the courier stops at each prop he has an order for. A supply's icon and count show over its prop when you are within 2 cells. The lobby Mirror hangs 2 cells east of the archway, behind the counter. `-kitchen` starts a run in the kitchen for a print (with `-shot out.png -at 0`, and `-ghost`). The kitchen's wall and floor tiles are cut and recolored by `python tools/kitchen_tiles.py`. Dev flags: `-change twin` starts a run with room 101 rolling to that type, mirrored, and room 102's change waiting on its guests, you in the corridor between them (use with `-shot out.png -at 28` for the reel, `-at 150` after it, `-ghost` for the ghost dimension); add `-view 1,1` to open the hotel view on a room and its actions, or `-term 1,0` to open the terminal on a tab and row (from 0), the last `1` pressing SPACE there.

## The hotel view

`Space` where it has nothing else to do opens the hotel view on the whole hotel, every room in sight (the fog lifts), each under its number and a change on its way (`101 > TWIN`), while the world runs on at a quarter speed. `WASD` picks a room (the camera moves half way in) and walks between rooms; `Space` opens the room's ring around it, one fixed slot per action: `Change type` on top (or a boarded room's `Room key`, alone), `Father K` left, `Deluxe` right, `Father M` lower left, `Room mirror` (then `Add mirror`) lower right; only the actions that apply now show, and it opens on the top slot, or the next action clockwise. `D`/`S` turn it clockwise, `A`/`W` back; the centre shows the action's name, price (red when you can't pay it; `Space` then shakes no) and effect. Everything is paid at once, never through the cart. `Change type` opens an inner ring of the other unlocked bed types; a waiting change shows there as `CANCEL > TWIN`, reached last. `Esc` steps back one level: beds, ring, room, hotel, play. Request pills show the room number and the item or a dirt splat, the same size in the view as in play. The terminal is only the store, in its "haunted shop" colors, and covers the whole screen: tabs on top (SUPPLIES, HOTEL, STAFF, CART), a list on the left that scrolls when it must, the picked item's detail on the right. Its bar shows the wallet and the Money and Ectoplasm earned per real minute (over the last game hour); the HUD counters hide while it's open. A locked item says what unlocks it; one you can't afford shows its price in red; what can't be bought any more (an unlock owned, the staff or the hotel at its max) isn't listed. Prints: `-view room,depth,pick -shot out.png -at 30` (depth -1 no room picked, 0 picked, 1 the ring, 2 the beds; use `-busy` or `-change twin` for a 3×3 or unlocked run, not `-play never`, whose walking keys turn the ring).

## Growth

The hall (the lobby and the kitchen) keeps one size, that of the two-column hotel, and stands centered under the rooms to the pixel: walk straight up from the middle of the entrance and you pass the middle of the archway and, with three columns, reach the middle room's door. Once the Mirror is owned the terminal's HOTEL tab sells `New column` (one more room on every floor, on the east) and `New floor` (a floor at the back), in Ectoplasm, each expansion 60 more than the last, times the per-room price factor (see "Prestige and saving"), one at a time, up to (3 + prestige level) a side: 3×3 on a first run. Place the order and the store closes: the world waits, the camera pulls back on the hotel, the new part slides in from off screen (a column from the east while the rooms move over above the hall; a floor from the north), and the camera comes back. Every room keeps its place, type, mood and guests; the new rooms arrive boarded up and need their keys. Prints: `-grow col -shot out.png -at 50` (the cinematic at frame 50: 20 pulled back, 50 and 64 sliding in, 130 done); `-grow col,floor` grows by a column at once and plays the floor's.

## Housekeepers

The terminal has a STAFF tab that sells the Housekeeper ($100 to hire, on sale once the hotel has earned $500, about minute 6 of a run; one for every 3 rooms owned, rounded up). She walks in from the street, dressed as a maid, and with nothing to do roams the whole hotel (hall, kitchen, corridors). When a room asks for a supply the stock has, she fetches it and carries it to the door (it pays as your own delivery does); when guests wait in the hall complaining of a dirty room, she cleans it. Each costs $60 a game day, first taken a day after she was hired. If the wallet is short on her payday she stops working and is owed the wage, taken the moment the money is there; if she is still owed at the next payday she walks out. Nothing on the HUD says so: she says it herself as you come near her ("No pay, no work."), warns you ahead when a payday is under 8 game hours away and the wallet can't cover it, and otherwise speaks only about one time in four. All numbers are placeholders until the economy tune. `go run . -staff 1,500` starts with one beside you in the kitchen, a full stock and $500; `-staff 2,0` starts with two already owed their wages.

## Walking, cleaning and lines

Everyone who walks looks three cells ahead and goes round whoever is there before they meet (staff, with no way round, pass through, so they never stand stuck); `go run . -busy` starts a stress run, a full 3x3 hotel with three housekeepers and a courier, to watch it. `Space` anywhere inside a room that has dirt to clean cleans it, so a stain cannot be missed. A guest says one line at a time. Ghosts have lines of their own (heard in the ghost dimension), never the time-of-day greetings, and they never sleep: they use no bed, day or night. In the hotel view the rooms not bought yet are veiled dark. Lines: the ones that only make sense in a room are never shown outside one; guests, ghosts and housekeepers have more of them, the courier and the priest now speak as you come near, and a housekeeper welcomes guests walking in.

## Two kinds of dirt

Guests make normal dirt, ghosts make spiritual dirt (each haunt, each fright). `Space` in a room cleans the kind of the dimension you are in: normal dirt in the real one, for nothing; spiritual dirt in the ghost one, for Ectoplasm. The other kind stays. Housekeepers clean only normal dirt; a priest clears spiritual dirt for money. Before the Mirror is bought there are no ghosts and so no spiritual dirt.

## Deluxe rooms

The HOTEL tab sells "Deluxe rooms" for 120 Ectoplasm once the hotel has grown at least once (the hotel expands first). After that every room's actions in the hotel view have a "Deluxe" row: one press, 40 Ectoplasm, and the room becomes the Deluxe version of the beds it has once it is empty. A Deluxe room charges about 1.6 times the rate ($200 a night for a single against $125) and stays Deluxe when its beds are changed.

## Passing on

A ghost has one haunt in it for each point of its Phantasm (1 to 12), the one it does as it settles in included. It dims a little with each, so how long it has left shows. Spent, it passes on: it rises off the floor, pales to white and is gone in two and a half seconds, and its room books the next ghost 20 seconds later. The weakest ghost haunts once and goes; a strong one outlasts a short stay and checks out as before. A pair passes on together, once its stronger one is spent. Only a haunt with a human in the room counts: a ghost alone lingers. Ghosts are seen only in the ghost dimension, so the passing is too; in the real one its Shadow fades from the wall.

## Pacing

The game is tuned so a player meets most of it in the first 10 minutes of real play, with no speed-up. Played in the agreed order (the first room, the Mirror, the second room, then everything that earns) the middle run of 16 reaches the Mirror at 0:35, the first Growth near minute 4, the first housekeeper near 7, the in-room Mirror near 5.5 and the first Deluxe upgrade near 9.5; the first Growth falls between 2:20 and 4:00 on every run measured (before ghosts passed on, a weak first ghost could push it to minute 8). What sets it: the Mirror costs $15, each key $15 more than the last, the first Growth 60 Ectoplasm (60 more each after), the in-room Mirror 120, Deluxe 120 and 40 a room, on sale only after the first Growth. A ghost haunts its room as it settles in and then every 4 game hours (40 s) counted from that moment, so the rooms' haunts are spread out and there is a harvest waiting somewhere most of the time. The concierge may spend 5 seconds in a room with its guests for free; lingering past that costs the room 1 Mood every 10 game minutes. `go run ./cmd/sim -strategy goals -days 4 -runs 16` prints when each milestone is reached and the longest stretch with nothing to buy and nothing to do.

## Priests

Each Father is a one-time unlock in the terminal's STAFF tab, paid in Ectoplasm (Father K 30, after $250 earned past the Mirror; Father M 80, after $750); only then does he show in a room's ring, and each call costs Ectoplasm (K 15, M 40; red when short). Father K halves the room's dirt. Father M also raises its Mood, banishes its Shadow and sends every ghost in it over, each paying at once the Ectoplasm its remaining haunts would have earned. Once K is unlocked, STAFF also sells his retainer (150 Ectoplasm a game day): he stays, does his ritual on the room with the most spiritual dirt, one after another, and paces the corridors when there is none; short on payday he leaves at once. Prices are placeholders.

## Mirrors and staff upgrades

The one-time Room mirror unlock costs money and Ectoplasm; after it, a room without a mirror gets `Add mirror` in its ring and the courier walks in and hangs one in place of the prop on that wall. A room with a mirror has a second ghost slot: a lone ghost books into it on its own and walks out of the mirror; Matching reads the higher Phantasm. Each New floor's corridor gets a covered mirror at a random room's front-wall corner; buy it at the HOTEL row (($100 and 60 Ectoplasm) times the number of corridor mirrors bought plus one, then 15% more for each room owned like a key, so the first costs $100 and 60 Ectoplasm with no rooms owned) and the courier walks there and uncovers it; then it shifts like the lobby's. Running shoes (STAFF, three tiers in Ectoplasm, once a housekeeper is hired) make every housekeeper, priest and the courier 15% faster per tier; an idle housekeeper sweeps the room with the most normal dirt, empty or sleeping rooms first. In the ghost dimension the concierge hears only ghost lines and never bothers guests by lingering. Brief one-line notices on the right, in the Ectoplasm counter's own text (`101 CLOSED`, `GUESTS FLED 102`), stack three at most and hide while the terminal is open.

## Matching

Every human party has a Nerve (6 to 12) and every ghost a Phantasm (1 to 12). A human fits a ghost whose Phantasm is within its Nerve. An arriving party books the free room whose ghost it fits best (the strongest within its Nerve), or, with none, the one it misses by least. A room with a human party and a ghost it fits is a good pair: the Shadow frightens them for Ectoplasm and they never flee. One it does not fit is a bad pair: the Shadow pays nothing, every haunt terrifies them, they step out on the second and flee on the third. In the hotel view small motes rise in a paired room, red for a bad pair, green and purple for a good one; nothing else shows it. A party in a bad pair pays half its hourly rent. To fix a bad pair, stand under the room's plate, beside its door, while its party is in: `TALK` slides in on the right of the HUD, in the Ectoplasm counter's style, and `Space` opens the guest's card; its `MOVE` shows when there is a room to offer (another open room whose ghost the party fits, empty or held by a party that could trade places) and starts the talk: the camera closes in on the two of you, the rest blurs, the world slows down and the guest stands and listens; the worse the pair (how far the ghost's Phantasm is beyond the party's Nerve), the slower the world (a quarter speed for a party that fits or barely misses, about a twelfth for the worst) and the heavier the blur, fixed for the talk. `A`/`D` picks the room, `W`/`S` an offer (only those the wallet can pay show; the plain ask always does), `Space` makes it, `Esc` leaves. The plain ask is free and usually refused; the discount costs a fifth of a night's Rate and is likely taken; "on the house" costs a night's Rate and is always taken. A scared guest and a nicer target room make the first two likelier. A refusal costs the guest's room 40 Mood and ends the talk, and `TALK` stays hidden for that guest for a game hour. An occupied target is a swap and the price covers both parties. On a yes the parties walk to their new rooms by themselves, their rent runs on at the new room's rate, and each room keeps its Mood. Ghosts cannot be moved. The HOTEL tab sells `Matchmaking` (40 Ectoplasm, once a housekeeper is hired): an idle housekeeper walks to a bad pair's door and pays the cheapest offer that is sure to be taken. All numbers are placeholders. Dev flag: `-match N` starts a run with a bad pair in room 101 and the reverse in 102, you in 101: `-match 0 -view 0 -shot out.png -at 40` prints the hotel view's frames, `-match 1 -shot out.png -at 70` the talk, `-match 2 -shot out.png -at 125` the swap under way.

## Prestige and saving

Once the hotel has grown to its limit and every room key is bought, Father P goes on sale in the terminal's STAFF tab (300 Ectoplasm, placeholder). SPACE on his row opens a card saying what you gain (the next level, a bigger hotel, the new bonus) and what you lose (the hotel, money, Ectoplasm, unlocks, staff); SPACE again pays and plays his cutscene: everyone gathers in the hall, ghosts too, Father P speaks, the world turns upside down, time races while everyone but him and all the furniture, whole, lifts off one after another and tumbles into a spiral that the spinning clock swallows, a white flash. The next run starts on Day 1 at 08:00 in the entrance hall, at the starting size, with no title or intro, and a notice gives the new level and bonus; every run start empties the notice feed, so none carries over. The first time the cutscene plays in full; after that SPACE skips to the flash. Each level raises the hotel's limit by one room a side and unlocks one new thing: level 1, the pantry, a HOTEL upgrade that stands a shelf of supplies in the middle of each corridor a new floor made (no stock of its own: with the concierge within 2 cells it marks the kitchen's stock, each supply's icon and `xN` side by side; SPACE there works only while a room asks for a supply the kitchen has, and takes the one asked by the nearest asking room). The bonus multiplies every Money and Ectoplasm earning, and each prestige adds to it: the square root of the run's Ectoplasm plus its unspent Money (donated, $10 for 1 Ectoplasm) over 6000. Room keys and growth cost 15% more for each room owned (linear), so a bigger hotel stays within reach. Tuned with the sim: a run takes about 30 real minutes to Father P at level 0 (all 8 sim runs get there, 25 to 33), 27 at level 1 (7 of 8, 21 to 31) and 32 at level 2 (8 of 8, 28 to 34), with `goals` never idle longer than 43 s (`go run ./cmd/sim -strategy goals -days 12 -runs 8 -level 1 -bonus 1.53`; level 0 drops `-level` and `-bonus`, level 2 is `-level 2 -bonus 2.17`). The terminal's header shows `LV N - xB` once there is one. The game saves at each dawn from Day 2 (the run: rooms, their types, Mood, dirt and mirrors, staff, money, Ectoplasm, unlocks) and right after a prestige (level and bonus); with a save the title offers only CONTINUE, which starts that morning with an empty hotel. On the web the save lives in the browser's local storage (`ghosthotel.save.v1`), on a computer in `ghosthotel/save.json` under the user config folder; clearing it is the only way to start over. Only a person's start reads or writes it: dev starts, prints, tests and the sim never do. Dev flags: `-prestige-ready 0` starts a full level-1 hotel on Father P's row (`1` with his card open); `-prestige` plays the cutscene in a busy hotel (with `-shot -at`: 300 gathering, 400 he appears, 600 lines, 850 the flip, 940 to 1250 the vortex round the clock, 1300 the flash); `-pantry` starts at level 1 with the pantry bought, after `-grow floor`. `-ritual k|m|kpace` loops a Father's ritual for tuning (K, M, or K on his retainer pacing the corridors) in room 101 with spiritual dirt that comes back, you inside its door in the ghost dimension (with `-shot -at`, or on the web `?args=-ritual k`); Father K's drawing and its knobs are in `game/ritual_k.go`. In the cutscene the speech bubbles and ask pills stay upright over the flipped hotel, and a flyer says nothing once it has lifted off. The vortex is `game/vortex.go`: each flyer (a character, a plant or pantry, the kitchen's props, a piece of furniture grouped from its `_rRcC` tiles, counters glued together) is drawn once into its own image and turned on the screen; walls, doors, windows, curtains and stairs stay.

## Guests and upgrades

Every guest has an identity, rolled on its own random stream so the simulation does not move: a human's name, age, job and a line; a ghost's name, death year, cause and a line. `TALK` at a room's plate (the brass plaque on the wall west of its door) opens the guest's card (a ghost's in the ghost dimension): the camera closes in on the guest, the world runs very slow, its line shows over its head, who it is on its left, what `REVEAL` read on its right and the options below; `A`/`D` flips a party of two, and its options are only those that apply: `REVEAL` (10 Ectoplasm, once a party) shows the guest's Nerve or Phantasm and its quirk, and `MOVE` is the talk. From the hotel's first growth on, about 1 guest in 3 has a quirk (human: Medium, Skeptic, Big tipper, Night owl, Neat freak; ghost: Show-off, Shy, Jealous, Gentle, Homebody; rules in `context/08-quests/cast.md`), and about 1 booking in 6 is one of the named cast: seven humans and three ghosts with their own lines, quirk and ghost kind, whose visits count across runs (saved; a revealed one stays revealed). The book on the entrance counter (`Space` beside it) gets a line from every party that leaves: cozy, dread or fled; passed-on or banished ghosts show only in the ghost dimension. Once his call is unlocked, about 1 solo booking in 20 is Father K or M as a guest: he pays three times the rate, his room sends its ghosts on without Ectoplasm and books none, and he scrubs its spiritual dirt each hour. Seven upgrades sit in the terminal's UPGRADES tab, each shown once it applies: `Space` puts the next level in the cart (one level of each at a time), you pay at checkout (the ghost-side three, Soundproofing, Seance table and Offering box, cost Ectoplasm as well as Money), and the courier brings it to the kitchen, where it counts from the drop: Lobby decor (+8% rate), Brochures (-20% wait between parties), Soundproofing (-20% Mood lost to haunts), Seance table (+20% Ectoplasm from sightings), Tip jar (+20% tips), Cleaning kit (-30% of a housekeeper's 20-game-minute clean) and Offering box (+80% of the 10 Ectoplasm each priest ritual pays); each level costs one step more and does 10% less than the last, and a prestige resets them. All numbers are placeholders. Dev flags: `-card N` opens a card at room 101's plate (0 a human, 1 revealed, 2 a party of two, 3 its ghost's, 4 Father K as a guest; with `-shot -at`); `-book 1` stands at the book, `-book 2` with its page open (`-ghost` for the ghost view). ## Dev tools

Simulation mode plays many Runs headless and in parallel, each by a Strategy that uses only the keys a player has, and prints a score per day (money plus Ectoplasm at `-ectorate`, default $0.5 a point: an assumed shop price), money per day, occupancy, Bookings, parties fled, Ectoplasm, serves, expired requests, priests called, their cost, Father M's calls (`M calls`), the days Father K was kept on his retainer (`retainer days`), the Ectoplasm spent on priests (`priest ecto`), room-hours closed, the game hours until the Mirror unlocked (`mirror at (h)`, mean over the runs that got it, `-` if none did), until the first room had its own Mirror (`room mirror at (h)`: median, then min..max, over the runs that got one) and invariant failures per Strategy. `grower` plays the opener and buys every expansion of the hotel it can pay for (the `growth` lines under the table give the game hour of each expansion and the rooms at the end); `never` stands at the terminal; `server` stays real and serves every room that asks; `keeper` also keeps the supplies stocked and cleans rooms' dirt in whichever dimension it's in; `smart` serves, cleans rooms in the ghost dimension and calls a weak priest to a closed room; `shopper` buys every unlock and priest on sale as soon as it can afford it, and plays `smart` in between. `goals` plays the agreed order of first goals, buys Father K's and Father M's call unlocks, calls them on the worst room and keeps Father K on his retainer; `matcher` plays `goals` and talks every bad pair it can into another room with the offer on the house, and `asker` does the same with the free plain ask whenever the guest will hear it (the dumb rule `matcher` is measured against); both buy Matchmaking when they can. Every run starts with the rooms closed, so `server`, `keeper` and `smart` first buy the Room keys; `never` buys nothing and so earns nothing; `opener` buys the first key, then the Mirror as soon as it can pay, then plays `smart` while it saves for Twin beds, changes a room to Twin and buys the in-Room Mirror for it:

```powershell
go run ./cmd/sim -strategy never,server,keeper,smart,shopper -runs 64 -days 7 -grid 2x1 -seed 1 -react 30 -keygap 6 -csv out.csv
```

`-react` and `-keygap` set the Pilot's pace in frames (`0` `0` is frame-perfect), `-nocheck` skips the invariants, `-csv` writes one row per Run, `-beds twin` starts every room with that bed layout (a normal run starts all single, so no pairs come until a room is changed). Run `i` uses seed `seed+i` for every Strategy. Any row replays on screen with `go run . -play smart -seed 7` (watch mode). `-grid 3x3` builds the hotel at that grid in a normal run too (rooms closed as ever), and combines with the dev starts (`-grid 3x3 -ghost -key 0`).

Prints: `go run . -shot out.png -at 2400` starts a Run, fast-forwards `-at` frames (600 a game hour) and saves one screen; `-ghost` starts any Run in the ghost dimension (not only with `-shot`). Dev starts combine (`-dirt 10 -ghosts 2 -ghost`, `-busy -kitchen`, …): one Run, started once; where two set the same thing (where the concierge stands, the wallet, every room's Mood) the later in `main.go`'s order wins. `-notices` stacks three notices in the feed; `-mirror` gives room 101 a mirror and two ghosts (use with `-ghost`) and room 102 the `Add mirror` action; `-corridors n` (with `-grow floor`) puts you beside the first corridor mirror, `n` already uncovered, with the money and Ectoplasm to buy one. `-father k` (or `m`) starts a run with rooms 101 and 102 open and guests in, both Fathers unlocked, Ectoplasm one short of both calls (the other call shows red), that Father already walking to room 101 and you in the Lobby Archway: `Space` opens the hotel view, and room 102's ring has no call for him until he walks out (one of each Father at a time); print it with `-father k -view 1,1 -shot out.png -at 60` (on `-grid 3x3` room 102 is `-view 7,1`).

## Web build

`sh tools/web.sh` builds `web/game.wasm`, gzips it to `game.wasm.gz` (15 MB to 3.8 MB; the page unpacks it itself, since itch.io sends no Content-Encoding), copies Go's `wasm_exec.js` next to it and writes `web/ghosthotel-web.zip` (index.html, wasm_exec.js and game.wasm.gz, 3.7 MB). On itch.io: Kind of project "HTML", upload the zip, tick "This file will be played in the browser", viewport 960x540. Click the game once so it takes the keyboard. On a touch-only device the page asks for a computer with a keyboard instead. To try it locally, `sh tools/serve.sh` serves `web/` in a 960x540 frame like itch's at http://localhost:8080/ (a frames and ticks readout shows at the top left during the live tests; `?nofps` hides it); file:// won't load the wasm. The crash guard is off on the web. `sh tools/wasmtest.sh [pkg ...] [-- -test.run X -test.bench Update]` runs the tests as wasm in Node with a small browser stub (all packages by default; about 7 minutes; package paths are `./game`, not `game`, and GOMAXPROCS must not be set, it fails with `newosproc not implemented`); the source-scanning guards skip there. `go test ./game -run '^$' -bench Update -benchmem` times one Update of a busy 3x3, 6x6, 8x8 and 10x10 hotel (about 0.05, 0.33, 1.1 and 1.5 ms native, with 95, 298, 410 and 385 allocations; 0.27, 0.9, 3.8 and 4.3 ms as wasm; the 10x10 has no budget), where `TestUpdateBudget` holds 6x6 under 2.5 ms and 8x8 under 5 ms as wasm. Walkers scan only nearby boxes (`game/near.go`), and `world.Path` is an A* read back to match Dijkstra's tie-break (`TestPathMatchesDijkstra`). `go test ./game -run '^$' -bench Draw -benchtime 200x -benchmem` times the CPU side of a drawn frame of a busy level-1 4x4 (real, ghost, a shift, a growth in ghost) and of a busy level-2 5x5 (real, ghost, the hotel view). Each prop knows its Room, so its dread and fog are no scan of the Rooms, and the pulled-back hotel view, where the world waits, redraws its whole-hotel picture every 10th frame (5x5 view: 6.3 to 0.8 ms of CPU a frame). The GPU side shows only in a browser: with `tools/serve.sh` running, `node tools/webprof.mjs "-level 1 -busy -shifts" 6` plays the web build in headless Chrome on the real GPU and prints its frame times (`--series` each frame, `--cpu` the busiest functions). Any of the game's flags reach the web build through the page's `?args=` (`/play?args=-busy -ghost`); `-level N` starts at a prestige level, `-shifts` shifts every 150 frames and `-growat N` adds a floor N frames in (`LOGS=1` prints the page's console). How the hotel is drawn, for speed: every prop picture (tiles, the walls' top band, the Mirror covers) is packed on one prop sheet when the map is new, and each run of props between two characters is one draw through a shader that applies the fog and dread tint, or the ghost palette; props off the picture are skipped; the floor is painted in one draw; characters and the other sprites have their ghost look baked once onto a few bake sheets; a growth draws the pulled-back hotel once for its slide; the map-sized textures (hotel view, darkness, floor) are kept across growths. Measured in Chrome (AMD Radeon integrated): a busy level-1 4x4 holds 60 FPS (p99 17 ms) in either dimension, shifting, and growing in either dimension, with one or two 33-83 ms frames as a growth begins (it was 8 FPS shifting and a crash growing in ghost; a dirty hotel growing in ghost had a 617 ms frame until stains moved with their Rooms).
