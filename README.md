# Ghost Hotel — vertical slice

Run from this directory:

```powershell
go run .
```

The executable's icon, a Shadow Person's black head, is `icon.ico`, linked in through `rsrc_windows_amd64.syso`; after changing the icon, rebuild that with `windres --preprocessor=cat -O coff -F pe-x86-64 icon.rc -o rsrc_windows_amd64.syso`.

Controls: `WASD` moves the concierge; `Space` opens the reception terminal (behind or in front of the counter), where `WASD` picks a room and `Space` calls a priest for it, and at the mirror on the lobby wall shifts the whole hotel between the real and the ghost dimension (through one of nine random 0.8 s transitions), at a booked room's door serves it in the real dimension; `Esc` closes the terminal, returns to title, or quits from it. Hold `T` to run the clock ×10 (dev aid).

How it plays: the hotel runs on a real-time clock, one in-game day in about 4 minutes, starting on Day 1 at 08:00. The hotel has two rooms side by side. Guests arrive already booked through an app, from the street during the day (none between 02:00 and 06:00, most from 14:00 to 22:00), and walk straight to their room; one comes only while a room it fits is empty. A party is one guest or a pair; a pair walks together, the second one a step behind, and needs a twin or double room. Every room opens as a Standard single ($125 a night); twin and double beds ($200) come by changing a room's type (see "Room types" below), and the Deluxe tier is a later unlock. The terminal pauses the world and shows each room's status, the money and the clock; there you pick a room and call a priest for it. Each guest stays 1–3 nights and is a homebody, who stays in, or a tourist, who goes out each morning (08:00–10:00) from the day after it arrives and comes back at night (19:00–22:00); the room stays booked while they're out. A door opens to whoever is near: the concierge, the courier and a priest open any room's door, a guest only its own, and a boarded room's stays shut to everyone. The concierge may walk in on guests (the darkness lifts while he is in), at a Mood cost for each game minute he stays. Once the whole party is inside with the door shut, the room fades into darkness; it clears only while its door is open, and stays dark while a tourist is out. The light follows the clock: clear by day, warm at dusk, deep blue at night under the corridors' ceiling lights, and pink at dawn. The lobby stays lit except from 01:00 to 06:00, and an empty room lights up while the concierge is in it. Your money shows on the terminal. On the last morning, between 10:00 and 12:00, the guest checks out: the whole stay is paid as it steps out of its door (`+$X`, the tip, scaled by the room's Mood pay factor) and the room is free again. At 23:00 guests walk into their beds, where only their heads show above the blanket, and get up at 07:00; the concierge can lie in a bed too. A bed is entered and left by its side, never over its foot: a guest walks round from its spot at the foot to the free cell beside the bed; in a double, against the wall, the wall-side sleeper crosses the other half. Awake in their room, guests keep busy: they look out of the windows, watch TV, browse the bookshelf, open the wardrobe or drawers, check the mirror or sit on the cushion, rest on their bed, stand at its foot glancing around, and a pair now and then stops to chat face to face. Guests in their room ask for room service once or twice a day while awake (07:00–23:00): a white `!!` balloon appears by their head, always on screen, pinned to the nearest edge when they're out of view. Serve them in the real dimension (SPACE at their door): the room's Mood rises by 25 when you get there at once, by less the longer they wait; when the request expires unanswered the Mood drops by 10. Serving doesn't calm fear. In the ghost dimension the hotel turns arcade-colored, you see through the darkness into booked rooms. Fear (a red glow) comes only from a Shadow Person's sightings, and each point of it earns a point of Ectoplasm (shown in green and purple, and on the terminal); cleaning dirt in the ghost dimension earns it too. A guest frightened enough flees with the whole party, unpaid, and the room is free at once: they jump with an `AAAH!` and run out at triple speed. Every guest has a look of its own: hair, skin and clothes are recolored at random. Guests talk in speech balloons: `Hello!` on arrival, a greeting by the time of day when going out or coming back (then a pair chats between themselves), and now and then a line in their room: less cozy the lower the room's Mood. You hear guests only near the concierge, and in the real dimension not through walls; a guest asking for service says nothing else. Each room also has a Mood, from -100 (dread) to 100 (cozy): guests leave mess that lowers it. In the ghost dimension the whole room shifts toward green and purple as it falls (in the real one it only tints faintly green), and at -100 it pulses: the room is closed and no new guest comes to it. A priest called at the terminal (Father K, $200, halves the dread; Father M, $500, clears it) walks in, works half an hour and lifts it. The terminal no longer sells a Shadow Person (the Hex is gone too); a Shadow (dev: `-shadow`) is a black figure that dashes along a room's back wall while guests are in, more often the lower the room's Mood. Each run frightens a guest who sees it (it hops and cries out) more than one who doesn't, and the shadow keeps the room uneasy, but it never scares anyone into fleeing. Father M banishes it.

Room Mood, how it works now: it is the one number that counts. It moves only on events and never drifts: serving a request raises it (+25, less the longer they waited), an expired request lowers it (-10), guests' mess, a frightened guest and the concierge lingering inside lower it, and cleaning raises it to +50 (a clean never lowers it). Dirt spots are Mood made visible: none from +50 up, then one for about every 3.75 points down to -100. Cleaning a room (SPACE on a stain, its guests in or not) wipes all its spots and, in the ghost dimension, banks half the dirt as Ectoplasm. Pay follows Mood through the Mood pay factor, 0 at -100, 1 at 0 and 2 at +100 (linear): it scales the rent and the checkout tip. Rent is paid up front, on each guest's own clock: the moment a guest steps into its room it pays its share of that hour (the Rate per 24 game hours, split between the party) as a `+$N` at the door, then again every game hour from that entry, in the room or out; a new run's rooms start at Mood +50; the tip, one night's Rate whatever the stay's length, is paid at checkout, so a dirty room pays little and a cozy one pays double. There is no per-guest comfort any more. Ghosts will also lower it by haunting (dev code: `g.haunts(room, damage)`; a ghost's Haunt stat x is the gap between haunts, its Phantasm sets the damage). Dev flag: `-mood N` starts a run with every room at Mood N, standing in room 101 (use with `-shot`).

Ghost guests, how they play now: a run starts with the Mirror locked, humans only; the Mirror on the lobby wall is drawn covered and SPACE at it does nothing; buy it at the terminal ($50, hotel tab), and once the courier carries it to the wall and installs it each room also books a ghost guest, next to its human one and independent of it: it walks in from the street on the ghost layer, one of the four characters redrawn as a flat arcade-ghost color by its kind (Wisp cyan, Specter pink, Wraith orange, Poltergeist red; which kind gets which is a placeholder), stays, sleeps and checks out like a guest. A ghost never bumps, yields or queues for a human, nor a human for a ghost. It pays no money, ignores dirt and asks for nothing; it only haunts: every few game hours in its room it lowers the room's Mood, by more the stronger it is (this is the first pressure on Mood; a closed room takes no ghost either). In the real dimension ghosts are not drawn. A strong ghost (every kind but the Wisp) switches on the Shadow People of whatever area it stands in, the room, a corridor or the entrance hall: the same Shadow as ever: a black figure that fades in on that area's upper wall: in a room it dashes along the back wall while humans are in, as often as its ghost haunts, and shows only while it dashes (only a guest who sees the dash is frightened; in a corridor or the hall now and then), frightening the humans who see it as before (they hop and cry out, Fear rises and earns Ectoplasm, never into fleeing by itself), under the room's fog like the props; Father M banishes it until that ghost leaves; it fades when the ghost leaves. The mirror prop inside a room is a separate upgrade (K1) and has nothing to do with the lobby Mirror: only the lobby one is drawn covered. In the ghost dimension ghosts draw like guests (one flat color each, almost opaque) and humans as flat white silhouettes with a small white glow. The haunt values are deliberately harsh placeholders (rooms close fast and money falls) until X2 lets you move parties between rooms. In the sim table, the new `ghosts` column counts ghost bookings per run; only the `opener` Strategy buys the Mirror, so ghosts show for the others only with `-mirror`, which starts every run with it unlocked. Dev flag: `-ghosts N` starts a run with a human and a ghost of Phantasm N (1..12; 4 and up is strong) settled in room 101, you inside it (use with `-shot out.png -at 9300` for them asleep, and `-ghost` for the ghost dimension).

The opening, how a run starts now: both rooms are closed and unowned: their doors are drawn boarded up, they book no guest of either dimension and the concierge cannot enter them, and the run starts with exactly $100, one Room key's worth; until a room is open the key is the only thing on sale, so no first buy can strand the run. The Mirror ($50) is bought from the first room's rent, on Day 1. Room keys are the `Room key` action of a room in the terminal's ROOMS tab (key icon), paid at once, and the courier walks the key to that room's door; when he drops it the room is owned, its door unboards and its first guest books about half an hour later. One key at a time, and the price rises with each room owned: $100 for the first, $200 for the second (`keyBase` and `keyStep`, placeholders), so the first choice is which room to open and the second room is earned. The Mirror stays covered until bought ($50), so a run goes: key, first guests, Mirror, then save up for the next key. The pacing goal is to reach the Mirror (the SHIFT feature) early: the `opener` Strategy does it in about 5 game hours on average (see `mirror at (h)` below); the money guests pay will be balanced later. The opening: the title screen is the whole hotel in the ghost dimension, both rooms boarded, the concierge not there and ghosts strolling the street. `Space` brings three brief lines over it, one after the other on a timer (`Space` shows them all at once); `Space` again and the concierge walks in from the street like a guest while the view closes in to the game's own, and the world shifts to real as he steps into the entrance; the clock and the world wait for all of it. Dev flags: `-skipintro` skips the intro when playing; the sim and every dev start (`-shot`, `-shop`, ...) never play it unless asked: `-intro N` starts at the opening (`0` the title, `1` the three lines, `2` the walk in; use with `-shot out.png -at F`), and `-key N` starts a run that has already ordered the key of room N (`0` is the first), you in the corridor by its door (use with `-shot -at 285` for the courier at the door, `-at 950` for the first guest). The sim starts every run closed too; `-open` starts it with every room owned, as before.

Room types, how they work now: a room's type is something you change, one room at a time. Twin beds, then the Double bed, are unlocks in the terminal's HOTEL tab ($350 and $700, each on sale once the one before is in and a little more money is earned: Twin after the Mirror). `Change type` is an action of a room in the ROOMS tab: it lists the unlocked beds, each with its fee ($50 Single, $75 Twin, $125 Double), and SPACE on one pays it at once. An empty room changes as soon as you close the terminal: it rolls like a slot machine's reel, the old room sliding out the bottom as the new one comes in from the top, in either dimension. A room in use is scheduled instead: it takes no new booking, human or ghost, until everyone has left (and the concierge and any priest are out of it), then rolls on its own, seen or not. A scheduled change shows on the room's card (`> TWIN`), and the room's `Change type` action becomes `CANCEL > TWIN`: SPACE cancels it for a full refund. The room keeps its Mood through a change; its dirt is spotted again on the new floor. Capacity and Rate follow the beds: a Twin or Double takes pairs. The in-Room Mirror (`Room mirror`, a room's action in the ROOMS tab, 90 Ectoplasm, bought once) is paid at once and carried by the courier to that room's door, like a key; when he gets there it is hung on the room's back wall, guests in or not, and a change the room waits for keeps waiting; from then on every room that changes type, and every room a key opens, gets one too, and every change also costs 5 Ectoplasm. SPACE under a room's Mirror shifts the dimension like the lobby's. A boarded room can't be changed. All amounts are placeholders. The supply props (fridge, counter, shelf) now stand against the lobby's upper wall, left of the Mirror, and are solid: you fetch from the cells just below them, where the courier drops the stock too. The pacing goal, the first room with its own Mirror at about 10 minutes of regular play (60 game hours), is not met yet: the `opener` gets there in 27 of 60 runs within 6 days, at 52 to 124 game hours, because a scheduled change waits out its guests' stays and a haunted room earns little. Dev flags: `-change twin` starts a run with room 101 rolling to that type, mirrored, and room 102's change waiting on its guests, you in the corridor between them (use with `-shot out.png -at 28` for the reel, `-at 150` after it, `-ghost` for the ghost dimension); add `-term 2,1,1` to open the terminal on a tab and row (from 0), the last `1` pressing SPACE there; on the ROOMS tab the row is a room (here: room 102's actions, with its change to cancel).

The ROOMS tab, how it works now: the terminal's tabs are SUPPLIES, HOTEL, ROOMS and CART. ROOMS is a grid of cards laid out like the hotel, one per room, and it grows with the hotel by itself. A card shows the room's number and beds, a change on its way (`> TWIN`), its own Mirror, its human guests' heads, the priest on it, its ghost guests' heads in their ghost color, its Shadow Person in black, and its Mood as a bar with no number (green from Mood 0 up, red below); a boarded room is dark, with its boarded door. Everything done to one room is bought on its card and paid at once, never through the cart: `Room key`, `Change type` (or cancelling it), `Father K`, `Father M`, `Room mirror`. Keys: `A`/`D` switches tabs; `W`, `S` or SPACE steps into the grid; `WASD` moves between rooms; SPACE opens the room's actions (`W`/`S`), SPACE buys one; a change of type takes one more SPACE on its beds; `ESC` steps back one level, and `W` from the grid's top row goes back to the tabs. The terminal stays open after a buy. SUPPLIES and HOTEL still go through the cart. Prints: `-term 2,0 -shot out.png -at 0` (the opening, both rooms boarded); with `-play never -grid 3x3 -change twin -ghosts 3` for a 3×3 hotel with guests.

The supplied Modern Interiors source pack remains untouched at the workspace root as requested. The `assets` package is the replacement boundary for future LimeZu sprite-sheet slicing; the current clean geometric presentation keeps the project runnable until those scattered files are curated. The W95FA font source is not present locally, so Ebitengine's built-in debug glyphs are used temporarily.

## Dev tools

Simulation mode plays many Runs headless and in parallel, each by a Strategy that uses only the keys a player has, and prints a score per day (money plus Ectoplasm at `-ectorate`, default $0.5 a point: an assumed shop price), money per day, occupancy, Bookings, parties fled, Ectoplasm, serves, expired requests, priests called, their cost, room-hours closed, the game hours until the Mirror unlocked (`mirror at (h)`, mean over the runs that got it, `-` if none did), until the first room had its own Mirror (`room mirror at (h)`: median, then min..max, over the runs that got one) and invariant failures per Strategy. `never` stands at the terminal; `server` stays real and serves every room that asks; `keeper` also keeps the supplies stocked and cleans rooms' dirt in whichever dimension it's in; `smart` serves, cleans rooms in the ghost dimension and calls a weak priest to a closed room; `shopper` buys every unlock and priest on sale as soon as it can afford it, and plays `smart` in between. Every run starts with the rooms closed, so `server`, `keeper` and `smart` first buy the Room keys; `never` buys nothing and so earns nothing; `opener` buys the first key, then the Mirror as soon as it can pay, then plays `smart` while it saves for Twin beds, changes a room to Twin and buys the in-Room Mirror for it:

```powershell
go run ./cmd/sim -strategy never,server,keeper,smart,shopper -runs 64 -days 7 -grid 2x1 -seed 1 -react 30 -keygap 6 -csv out.csv
```

`-react` and `-keygap` set the Pilot's pace in frames (`0` `0` is frame-perfect), `-nocheck` skips the invariants, `-csv` writes one row per Run, `-beds twin` starts every room with that bed layout (a normal run starts all single, so no pairs come until a room is changed). Run `i` uses seed `seed+i` for every Strategy. Any row replays on screen with `go run . -play smart -seed 7` (watch mode).

Prints: `go run . -shot out.png -at 2400` starts a Run, fast-forwards `-at` frames (600 a game hour) and saves one screen; `-ghost` starts it in the ghost dimension.
