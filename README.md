# Ghost Hotel — vertical slice

Run from this directory:

```powershell
go run .
```

The executable's icon, a Shadow Person's black head, is `icon.ico`, linked in through `rsrc_windows_amd64.syso`; after changing the icon, rebuild that with `windres --preprocessor=cat -O coff -F pe-x86-64 icon.rc -o rsrc_windows_amd64.syso`.

Controls: `WASD` moves the concierge; `Space` opens the reception terminal (behind or in front of the counter), where `WASD` picks a room and `Space` calls a priest for it, and at the mirror on the lobby wall shifts the whole hotel between the real and the ghost dimension (through one of nine random 0.8 s transitions), at a booked room's door serves it in the real dimension; `Esc` closes the terminal, returns to title, or quits from it. Hold `T` to run the clock ×10 (dev aid).

How it plays: the hotel runs on a real-time clock, one in-game day in about 4 minutes, starting on Day 1 at 08:00. The hotel has two rooms side by side. Guests arrive already booked through an app, from the street during the day (none between 02:00 and 06:00, most from 14:00 to 22:00), and walk straight to their room; one comes only while a room it fits is empty. A party is one guest or a pair; a pair walks together, the second one a step behind, and needs a twin or double room. Rooms are Standard or Deluxe, with a single bed ($25 / $40 a night) or twin or double beds ($40 / $64), drawn when the run starts. The terminal pauses the world and shows each room's status, the money and the clock; there you pick a room and call a priest for it. Each guest stays 1–3 nights and is a homebody, who stays in, or a tourist, who goes out each morning (08:00–10:00) from the day after it arrives and comes back at night (19:00–22:00); the room stays booked while they're out, and the concierge can never enter a booked room. Once the whole party is inside with the door shut, the room fades into darkness; it clears only while its door is open, and stays dark while a tourist is out. The light follows the clock: clear by day, warm at dusk, deep blue at night under the corridors' ceiling lights, and pink at dawn. The lobby stays lit except from 01:00 to 06:00, and an empty room lights up while the concierge is in it. Your money shows on the terminal. On the last morning, between 10:00 and 12:00, the guest checks out: the whole stay is paid as it steps out of its door (`+$X`, the tip, scaled by the room's Mood pay factor) and the room is free again. At 23:00 guests walk into their beds, where only their heads show above the blanket, and get up at 07:00; the concierge can lie in an empty room's bed too. Awake in their room, guests keep busy: they look out of the windows, watch TV, browse the bookshelf, open the wardrobe or drawers, check the mirror or sit on the cushion, rest on their bed, stand at its foot glancing around, and a pair now and then stops to chat face to face. Guests in their room ask for room service once or twice a day while awake (07:00–23:00): a white `!!` balloon appears by their head, always on screen, pinned to the nearest edge when they're out of view. Serve them in the real dimension (SPACE at their door): the room's Mood rises by 25 when you get there at once, by less the longer they wait; when the request expires unanswered the Mood drops by 10. Serving doesn't calm fear. In the ghost dimension the hotel turns arcade-colored, you see through the darkness into booked rooms. Fear (a red glow) comes only from a Shadow Person's sightings, and each point of it earns a point of Ectoplasm (shown in green and purple, and on the terminal); cleaning dirt in the ghost dimension earns it too. A guest frightened enough flees with the whole party, unpaid, and the room is free at once: they jump with an `AAAH!` and run out at triple speed. Every guest has a look of its own: hair, skin and clothes are recolored at random. Guests talk in speech balloons: `Hello!` on arrival, a greeting by the time of day when going out or coming back (then a pair chats between themselves), and now and then a line in their room: less cozy the lower the room's Mood. You hear guests only near the concierge, and in the real dimension not through walls; a guest asking for service says nothing else. Each room also has a Mood, from -100 (dread) to 100 (cozy): guests leave mess that lowers it. In the ghost dimension the whole room shifts toward green and purple as it falls (in the real one it only tints faintly green), and at -100 it pulses: the room is closed and no new guest comes to it. A priest called at the terminal (Father K, $40, halves the dread; Father M, $100, clears it) walks in, works half an hour and lifts it. The terminal no longer sells a Shadow Person (the Hex is gone too); a Shadow (dev: `-shadow`) is a black figure that dashes along a room's back wall while guests are in, more often the lower the room's Mood. Each run frightens a guest who sees it (it hops and cries out) more than one who doesn't, and the shadow keeps the room uneasy, but it never scares anyone into fleeing. Father M banishes it.

Room Mood, how it works now: it is the one number that counts. It moves only on events and never drifts: serving a request raises it (+25, less the longer they waited), an expired request lowers it (-10), guests' mess, a frightened guest and the concierge lingering inside lower it, and cleaning raises it to +50 (a clean never lowers it). Dirt spots are Mood made visible: none from +50 up, then one for about every 3.75 points down to -100. Cleaning a room (SPACE on a stain while it is empty) wipes all its spots and, in the ghost dimension, banks half the dirt as Ectoplasm. Pay follows Mood through the Mood pay factor, 0 at -100, 1 at 0 and 2 at +100 (linear): it scales the rent and the checkout tip. Rent is paid continuously: each guest inside its room pays its share of the Rate per 24 game hours every frame, so the money counter starts climbing the moment a guest steps in (a `+$N` still floats at the door each game hour); the tip, 10% of the stay, is paid at checkout, so a dirty room pays little and a cozy one pays double. There is no per-guest comfort any more. Ghosts will also lower it by haunting (dev code: `g.haunts(room, damage)`; a ghost's Haunt stat x is the gap between haunts, its Phantasm sets the damage). Dev flag: `-mood N` starts a run with every room at Mood N, standing in room 101 (use with `-shot`).

Ghost guests, how they play now: a run starts with the Mirror locked, humans only; the Mirror on the lobby wall is drawn covered and SPACE at it does nothing; buy it at the terminal ($10, hotel tab), and once the courier carries it to the wall and installs it each room also books a ghost guest, next to its human one and independent of it: it walks in from the street on the ghost layer, one of the four characters redrawn as a flat arcade-ghost color by its kind (Wisp cyan, Specter pink, Wraith orange, Poltergeist red; which kind gets which is a placeholder), stays, sleeps and checks out like a guest. A ghost never bumps, yields or queues for a human, nor a human for a ghost. It pays no money, ignores dirt and asks for nothing; it only haunts: every few game hours in its room it lowers the room's Mood, by more the stronger it is (this is the first pressure on Mood; a closed room takes no ghost either). In the real dimension ghosts are not drawn. A strong ghost (every kind but the Wisp) switches on the Shadow People of whatever area it stands in, the room, a corridor or the entrance hall: the same Shadow as ever: a black figure that fades in on that area's upper wall and dashes along it (in a room while humans are in, more often the lower its Mood; in a corridor or the hall now and then), frightening the humans who see it as before (they hop and cry out, Fear rises and earns Ectoplasm, never into fleeing by itself), under the room's fog like the props; Father M banishes it until that ghost leaves; it fades when the ghost leaves. The mirror prop inside a room is a separate upgrade (K1) and has nothing to do with the lobby Mirror: only the lobby one is drawn covered. In the ghost dimension ghosts draw like guests (one flat color each, almost opaque) and humans as flat white silhouettes with a small white glow. A room's door is shut to the concierge only while guests of his own dimension are inside. The haunt values are deliberately harsh placeholders (rooms close fast and money falls) until X2 lets you move parties between rooms. In the sim table, the new `ghosts` column counts ghost bookings per run; only the `opener` Strategy buys the Mirror, so ghosts show for the others only with `-mirror`, which starts every run with it unlocked. Dev flag: `-ghosts N` starts a run with a human and a ghost of Phantasm N (1..12; 4 and up is strong) settled in room 101, you inside it (use with `-shot out.png -at 9300` for them asleep, and `-ghost` for the ghost dimension).

The opening, how a run starts now: both rooms are closed and unowned: their doors are drawn boarded up, they book no guest of either dimension and the concierge cannot enter them, and the run starts with exactly $20, one Room key's worth; until a room is open the key is the only thing on sale, so no first buy can strand the run. The Mirror ($10) is bought from the first room's rent, on Day 1. Room keys are the new `Room key` row of the terminal's SERVICES tab (key icon): it asks for the room like the priests do, and the courier walks the key to that room's door; when he drops it the room is owned, its door unboards and its first guest books about half an hour later. One key at a time, and the price rises with each room owned: $20 for the first, $40 for the second (`keyBase` and `keyStep`, placeholders), so the first choice is which room to open and the second room is earned. The Mirror stays covered until bought ($10), so a run goes: key, first guests, Mirror, then save up for the next key. The pacing goal is to reach the Mirror (the SHIFT feature) early: the `opener` Strategy does it in about 5 game hours on average (see `mirror at (h)` below); the money guests pay will be balanced later. The opening: the title screen is the whole hotel in the ghost dimension, both rooms boarded, the concierge not there and ghosts strolling the street. `Space` brings three brief lines over it, one after the other on a timer (`Space` shows them all at once); `Space` again and the concierge walks in from the street like a guest while the view closes in to the game's own, and the world shifts to real as he steps into the entrance; the clock and the world wait for all of it. Dev flags: `-skipintro` skips the intro when playing; the sim and every dev start (`-shot`, `-shop`, ...) never play it unless asked: `-intro N` starts at the opening (`0` the title, `1` the three lines, `2` the walk in; use with `-shot out.png -at F`), and `-key N` starts a run that has already ordered the key of room N (`0` is the first), you in the corridor by its door (use with `-shot -at 285` for the courier at the door, `-at 950` for the first guest). The sim starts every run closed too; `-open` starts it with every room owned, as before.

The supplied Modern Interiors source pack remains untouched at the workspace root as requested. The `assets` package is the replacement boundary for future LimeZu sprite-sheet slicing; the current clean geometric presentation keeps the project runnable until those scattered files are curated. The W95FA font source is not present locally, so Ebitengine's built-in debug glyphs are used temporarily.

## Dev tools

Simulation mode plays many Runs headless and in parallel, each by a Strategy that uses only the keys a player has, and prints a score per day (money plus Ectoplasm at `-ectorate`, default $0.5 a point: an assumed shop price), money per day, occupancy, Bookings, parties fled, Ectoplasm, serves, expired requests, priests called, their cost, room-hours closed, the game hours until the Mirror unlocked (`mirror at (h)`, mean over the runs that got it, `-` if none did) and invariant failures per Strategy. `never` stands at the terminal; `server` stays real and serves every room that asks; `keeper` also keeps the supplies stocked and cleans empty rooms' dirt in whichever dimension it's in; `smart` serves, cleans empty rooms in the ghost dimension and calls a weak priest to a closed room; `shopper` buys every unlock and priest on sale as soon as it can afford it, and plays `smart` in between. Every run starts with the rooms closed, so `server`, `keeper` and `smart` first buy the Room keys; `never` buys nothing and so earns nothing; `opener` buys the first key, then the Mirror as soon as it can pay, then plays `smart`:

```powershell
go run ./cmd/sim -strategy never,server,keeper,smart,shopper -runs 64 -days 7 -grid 2x1 -seed 1 -react 30 -keygap 6 -csv out.csv
```

`-react` and `-keygap` set the Pilot's pace in frames (`0` `0` is frame-perfect), `-nocheck` skips the invariants, `-csv` writes one row per Run. Run `i` uses seed `seed+i` for every Strategy. Any row replays on screen with `go run . -play smart -seed 7` (watch mode).

Prints: `go run . -shot out.png -at 2400` starts a Run, fast-forwards `-at` frames (600 a game hour) and saves one screen; `-ghost` starts it in the ghost dimension.
