# Ghost Hotel — vertical slice

Run from this directory:

```powershell
go run .
```

The executable's icon, a Shadow Person's black head, is `icon.ico`, linked in through `rsrc_windows_amd64.syso`; after changing the icon, rebuild that with `windres --preprocessor=cat -O coff -F pe-x86-64 icon.rc -o rsrc_windows_amd64.syso`.

Controls: `WASD` moves the concierge; `Space` opens the reception terminal (behind or in front of the counter), where `WASD` picks a room and `Space` calls a priest for it, and at the mirror on the lobby wall shifts the whole hotel between the real and the ghost dimension (through one of nine random 0.8 s transitions), at a booked room's door serves it in the real dimension; `Esc` closes the terminal, returns to title, or quits from it. Hold `T` to run the clock ×10 (dev aid).

How it plays: the hotel runs on a real-time clock, one in-game day in about 4 minutes, starting on Day 1 at 08:00. The hotel has two rooms side by side. Guests arrive already booked through an app, from the street during the day (none between 02:00 and 06:00, most from 14:00 to 22:00), and walk straight to their room; one comes only while a room it fits is empty. A party is one guest or a pair; a pair walks together, the second one a step behind, and needs a twin or double room. Rooms are Standard or Deluxe, with a single bed ($25 / $40 a night) or twin or double beds ($40 / $64), drawn when the run starts. The terminal pauses the world and shows each room's status, the money and the clock; there you pick a room and call a priest for it. Each guest stays 1–3 nights and is a homebody, who stays in, or a tourist, who goes out each morning (08:00–10:00) and comes back at night (19:00–22:00); the room stays booked while they're out, and the concierge can never enter a booked room. Once the whole party is inside with the door shut, the room fades into darkness; it clears only while its door is open, and stays dark while a tourist is out. The light follows the clock: clear by day, warm at dusk, deep blue at night under the corridors' ceiling lights, and pink at dawn. The lobby stays lit except from 01:00 to 06:00, and an empty room lights up while the concierge is in it. Your money shows on the terminal. On the last morning, between 10:00 and 12:00, the guest checks out: the whole stay is paid as it steps out of its door (`+$X`, the tip, scaled by the room's Mood pay factor) and the room is free again. At 23:00 guests walk into their beds, where only their heads show above the blanket, and get up at 07:00; the concierge can lie in an empty room's bed too. Awake in their room, guests keep busy: they look out of the windows, watch TV, browse the bookshelf, open the wardrobe or drawers, check the mirror or sit on the cushion, rest on their bed, stand at its foot glancing around, and a pair now and then stops to chat face to face. Guests in their room ask for room service once or twice a day while awake (07:00–23:00): a white `!!` balloon appears by their head, always on screen, pinned to the nearest edge when they're out of view. Serve them in the real dimension (SPACE at their door): the room's Mood rises by 25 when you get there at once, by less the longer they wait; when the request expires unanswered the Mood drops by 10. Serving doesn't calm fear. In the ghost dimension the hotel turns arcade-colored, you see through the darkness into booked rooms. Fear (a red glow) comes only from a Shadow Person's sightings, and each point of it earns a point of Ectoplasm (shown in green and purple, and on the terminal); cleaning dirt in the ghost dimension earns it too. A guest frightened enough flees with the whole party, unpaid, and the room is free at once: they jump with an `AAAH!` and run out at triple speed. Every guest has a look of its own: hair, skin and clothes are recolored at random. Guests talk in speech balloons: `Hello!` on arrival, a greeting by the time of day when going out or coming back (then a pair chats between themselves), and now and then a line in their room: less cozy the lower the room's Mood. You hear guests only near the concierge, and in the real dimension not through walls; a guest asking for service says nothing else. Each room also has a Mood, from -100 (dread) to 100 (cozy): guests leave mess that lowers it. In the ghost dimension the whole room shifts toward green and purple as it falls (in the real one it only tints faintly green), and at -100 it pulses: the room is closed and no new guest comes to it. A priest called at the terminal (Father K, $40, halves the dread; Father M, $100, clears it) walks in, works half an hour and lifts it. The terminal no longer sells a Shadow Person (the Hex is gone too); a Shadow (dev: `-shadow`) is a black figure that dashes along a room's back wall while guests are in, more often the lower the room's Mood. Each run frightens a guest who sees it (it hops and cries out) more than one who doesn't, and the shadow keeps the room uneasy, but it never scares anyone into fleeing. Father M banishes it.

Room Mood, how it works now: it is the one number that counts. It moves only on events and never drifts: serving a request raises it (+25, less the longer they waited), an expired request lowers it (-10), guests' mess, a frightened guest and the concierge lingering inside lower it, and cleaning raises it to +50 (a clean never lowers it). Dirt spots are Mood made visible: none from +50 up, then one for about every 3.75 points down to -100. Cleaning a room (SPACE on a stain while it is empty) wipes all its spots and, in the ghost dimension, banks half the dirt as Ectoplasm. Pay follows Mood through the Mood pay factor, 0 at -100, 1 at 0 and 2 at +100 (linear): it scales the hourly money and the checkout tip, so a dirty room pays little and a cozy one pays double. There is no per-guest comfort any more. Ghosts will also lower it by haunting (dev code: `g.haunts(room, damage)`; a ghost's Haunt stat x is the gap between haunts, its Phantasm sets the damage). Dev flag: `-mood N` starts a run with every room at Mood N, standing in room 101 (use with `-shot`).

Ghost guests, how they play now: once the Mirror is unlocked (until a new game sets its start state, it always is) each room also books a ghost guest, next to its human one and independent of it: it walks in from the street on the ghost layer, one of the four characters shifted to a hue by its kind (Wisp cyan, Specter violet, Wraith green, Poltergeist rose; all placeholders), stays, sleeps and checks out like a guest. A ghost never bumps, yields or queues for a human, nor a human for a ghost. It pays no money, ignores dirt and asks for nothing; it only haunts: every few game hours in its room it lowers the room's Mood, by more the stronger it is (this is the first pressure on Mood; a closed room takes no ghost either). In the real dimension ghosts are not drawn. A strong ghost (every kind but the Wisp) switches on the Shadow People of whatever area it stands in, the room, a corridor or the entrance hall: a dark figure on that area's upper wall, wherever the ghost stands, that fades when it leaves. In the ghost dimension ghosts draw like guests (hued, semi-transparent) and humans as dim silhouettes. A room's door is shut to the concierge only while guests of his own dimension are inside. In the sim table, the new `ghosts` column counts ghost bookings per run. Dev flag: `-ghosts N` starts a run with a human and a ghost of Phantasm N (1..12; 4 and up is strong) settled in room 101, you inside it (use with `-shot out.png -at 9300` for them asleep, and `-ghost` for the ghost dimension).

The supplied Modern Interiors source pack remains untouched at the workspace root as requested. The `assets` package is the replacement boundary for future LimeZu sprite-sheet slicing; the current clean geometric presentation keeps the project runnable until those scattered files are curated. The W95FA font source is not present locally, so Ebitengine's built-in debug glyphs are used temporarily.

## Dev tools

Simulation mode plays many Runs headless and in parallel, each by a Strategy that uses only the keys a player has, and prints a score per day (money plus Ectoplasm at `-ectorate`, default $0.5 a point: an assumed shop price), money per day, occupancy, Bookings, parties fled, Ectoplasm, serves, expired requests, priests called, their cost, room-hours closed and invariant failures per Strategy. `never` stands at the terminal; `server` stays real and serves every room that asks; `keeper` also keeps the supplies stocked and cleans empty rooms' dirt in whichever dimension it's in; `smart` serves, cleans empty rooms in the ghost dimension and calls a weak priest to a closed room; `shopper` buys every unlock and priest on sale as soon as it can afford it, and plays `smart` in between:

```powershell
go run ./cmd/sim -strategy never,server,keeper,smart,shopper -runs 64 -days 7 -grid 2x1 -seed 1 -react 30 -keygap 6 -csv out.csv
```

`-react` and `-keygap` set the Pilot's pace in frames (`0` `0` is frame-perfect), `-nocheck` skips the invariants, `-csv` writes one row per Run. Run `i` uses seed `seed+i` for every Strategy. Any row replays on screen with `go run . -play smart -seed 7` (watch mode).

Prints: `go run . -shot out.png -at 2400` starts a Run, fast-forwards `-at` frames (600 a game hour) and saves one screen; `-ghost` starts it in the ghost dimension.
